04 Format 01 · SHORT FILMS

Cinematic short films where
travel plays a supporting role.

⏱ 1 minute, 1.5 minutes max

Conversations between friends, sometimes just 2 people and sometimes a whole group. Nothing is sold. The viewer gets hooked on the conversation, and the trip becomes their idea.

Stories that hit home, and conversations about places so unbelievable you just want to go there.

01

Stories that hit home

Relatable, sometimes emotional. The trip with your parents that keeps getting pushed. The saved folder you never visit. The colleague who's somehow always on holiday. The viewer sees their own life, and the trip is the way out.

02

Places you won't believe

Banter, curiosity, wanderlust. A real place that sounds made up: a town where you can't die, a village with no doors, a country near Australia where Diwali is a national holiday. 1 friend refuses to believe it, the other knows the story, and by the end they're booking.

★ Why it works

Nobody likes being sold to. Everyone likes being understood.The feed has trained people to skip anything that looks like an ad. A son who keeps saying "baad mein le jaunga" doesn't feel like an ad. It feels like you.

Conversational scripts build identity.Real conversations, natural Hinglish and cinematic frames turn a deals page into a brand people recognise: the one that understands why Indians travel, from the parents' first trip to the trip the friend group never takes.

It gets shared, not just watched.Stories spread through DMs: "bhai ye tu hai", tagging the friend who keeps cancelling. That reach is earned, and the talking head never gets it.

💬 Fixes the comment problem

Stories make people talk, not just type a keyword.

Right now most comments are 1-word keyword replies. A story people see themselves in pulls out real replies: they share their own version, they get emotional, and they tag the friend the film is about. That's conversation, and conversation is what the algorithm rewards.

Short films · Why Hinglish

SHORT FILMS speak the language our audience actually talks in.

SHORT FILMS are written in Hinglish, on purpose. (THE GAME stays in English, for quick, universal play-along.)

Broader reachPages covering the same topics we've covered, in Hinglish, reached far more people than our English versions. Same idea, bigger audience.
It still reads tier 1Cinematic framing, real writing and premium visuals keep it aspirational. Hinglish is how urban India speaks to friends, so the audience stays the one Cleartrip wants.
It feels realNobody argues with their best friend in textbook English. The emotion only lands if the dialogue sounds like a real conversation.
Better targeted, not just biggerBeautiful short films in the language people think in. No other travel brand is doing this, so Cleartrip owns it.

05 Format 02 · THE GAME

The game: a travel quiz
built for dopamine.

⏱ 45–60 seconds max

Kadesh and Minal. A 45–60 second timer. A live score over their heads. Stakes that get worse with every wrong answer, and a punishment waiting for the loser. Nobody wants a lesson every day. Everyone wants to see who gets the ice bucket.

⚡ Why it works

Built for dopamine, retention and today's attention span. It talks about travel without talking about travel.

Stakes in frame 1, a ticking clock and a score that keeps swinging: the viewer's brain has a reason to stay till the last second. They play along, so it feels like a game, not content. Travel comes in through the questions, so nobody feels like they're getting gyaan.

Every episode, same engine

0sFrame 1 shows the stakes. The 45–60s timer is already running.
Q1–4An off-camera voice reads each question. 3 options pop up. +1 right, −1 wrong, the score jumps over their heads.
Q5⏸ Everything freezes: "YOUR TURN. 3 SECONDS." The viewer plays.
Q6–8Pop culture × travel, crazy country facts, and 1 rage-bait answer people will argue about.
0:00Loser pays the punishment. A tie goes to 1 sudden-death question.
EndA bonus question with no answer on screen. "Comment yours, real answer pinned tomorrow."

Built to start arguments.Hawaiian pizza is from Canada. Chicken tikka masala is claimed by Glasgow. Every answer is true, and every one gets an "um, actually".

Built to scale.12 reusable stakes, each with its own question set. 5 episodes shoot in a day, in an office or garden.

CLEARTRIP in every episode.At least 1 question per episode is a Cleartrip question: a live fare ("Goa or Bangkok, which is cheaper from Delhi this Saturday?"), a sale price or an app feature. The product is part of the game, not an ad break.

Gives YOUTUBE a real boost.Quiz-and-punishment is one of the formats YouTube Shorts rewards most: high watch-through, replays and comment wars. Every episode goes up on Shorts too, so the game becomes the engine that grows Cleartrip's YouTube views alongside Instagram, from the same shoot.

💬 Fixes the comment problem

Every episode ends in a debate in the comments.

Surprising-but-true answers, a rage-bait question and a bonus with no answer on screen give people something to argue about. They post their scores, defend their answers and tag a friend to beat them. That's debate, not a 1-word reply.

⏸ Audience

Q5, the 3-second audience question. Everything freezes and the viewer plays.

Sudden death

Only if it's a tie at 0:00. 1 final question, the hardest in the set. First right answer wins; the other takes the punishment. Not tied? It's skipped.

Bonus

Shown after the punishment, never answered on camera. "Comment your answer. Real answer in the pinned comment tomorrow." Often opinion or several right answers, so people argue.
